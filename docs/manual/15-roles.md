# 15. The six roles

Roles are the security model. Every screen and every action asks the role first,
so what you can see and what you can press are both decided here.

There is no sign-in in this phase. You choose a role from the session menu, and
the whole product behaves as that role until you choose another. The opener of
this manual has the steps.

## The six

| Role                    | What they do                                             |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Administrator           | Full access, including users, roles and settings.        |
| Facility manager        | Runs one or more sites: assets, work, plans and reports. |
| Maintenance coordinator | Plans and assigns the daily work across trades.          |
| Technician              | Carries out assigned work orders on site.                |
| Inspector               | Completes inspections and raises findings.               |
| Auditor                 | Reads records and reports. Changes nothing.              |

## Four rules worth knowing

**Assigning and cancelling are held apart from doing the work.** A technician
may finish a job and log the time, but may not hand work out or call it off.
Those are decisions about the schedule, and cancelling is the one act on an
order that cannot be undone.

**Issuing a part and correcting a count are two separate rights**, even though
the same four roles hold both. Issuing takes a known quantity out against a job.
Correcting overrides the recorded count with what was found on the shelf, which
can move stock either way.

**Attaching a document reaches the technician and the inspector.** The person
standing at the machine is usually the person with the photograph, and paperwork
that has to wait for a coordinator arrives late or not at all.

**Reading is decided by the screen, changing by the action.** A role that can
open a screen can read everything on it. Whether they can change anything is a
separate question, asked button by button.

## What a refusal looks like

Two different things, on purpose.

**A whole screen.** You get a panel headed **Not available to your role**, then
"This screen is limited to other roles. Your current role is" and your role
name, and underneath "Switch role from the session menu to see it."

**A single button.** It stays where it is, readable and switched off. Hover over
it or reach it with the keyboard and it says your role name, then "cannot do
this. Switch role to continue."

Buttons are never hidden. A missing button tells you nothing — you cannot tell
whether the feature is absent or whether you simply may not use it. A button
that is present and says why answers both questions at once.

## Seeing the product as somebody else

1. Press the session button at the top right, showing your current role.
2. Under **Switch role**, pick another. Each is listed with its one-line
   description.
3. A message says "Now viewing as" and the new role.

If the new role may open the screen you are on, you stay there — which is the
fastest way to see the same screen through two sets of eyes. If it may not, you
land on that role's home: the Dashboard, or My work today.
